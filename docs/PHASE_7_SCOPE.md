# Phase 7 — Tournament & Table Intelligence

Status: complete.

## Goal

Complete the largest remaining curriculum gaps while preserving RiverMind's
local-first, evidence-based coaching loop. Phase 7 teaches tournament stack
pressure, practical poker math, and opponent adjustments, then connects those
concepts to randomized practice and real table decisions.

## Slice 1 — Tournament foundations (implemented)

- Add a dedicated Tournament chapter without adding another top-level tab.
- Teach stack zones, efficient short-stack opening, reshoves, and disciplined
  calls against all-ins.
- Add a focused short-stack practice pack with independently randomized
  decision families across three-player and six-player contexts.
- Feed tournament evidence into the personal plan, adaptive recommendations,
  spaced review, progress insights, and chapter mastery.

## Slice 2 — Bubble pressure and introductory ICM (implemented)

- Explain risk premium, stack coverage, and why chip value changes near payouts.
- Add practical bubble decisions for leaders, middle stacks, and short stacks.
- Keep every recommendation framed as a learning baseline rather than a
  solver-perfect tournament chart.

## Slice 3 — Opponent reads and adjustments (implemented)

- Teach how to recognize RiverMind's patient, balanced, sticky, pressure, and
  deceptive styles from repeated evidence.
- Add exploit practice for excessive calling, folding, and aggression.
- Require a meaningful sample before presenting a tendency as reliable.

## Slice 4 — Deeper poker math (implemented)

- Add implied odds, reverse implied odds, and break-even bluff percentages.
- Connect each calculation to a practical call, bluff, or sizing decision.
- Keep the table math concise enough to use during live play.

## Slice 5 — Applied tournament and read missions (implemented)

- Add short table missions that grade only the tagged tournament or adjustment
  decisions.
- Carry mission evidence into session coaching and the next personal plan.
- Link weak decisions back to the exact lesson and practice family.

## Slice 6 — Content UX and release polish (implemented)

- Keep the personal plan primary while the larger catalog stays optional.
- Make prerequisites, difficulty, mastery evidence, and next steps clear.
- Complete English, Simplified Chinese, and Traditional Chinese content.
- Pass small-phone, reduced-motion, accessibility, and full release gates.

## Deferred

- Accounts and cross-device history sync
- Friends, realtime tables, public leaderboards, and social sharing
- Nine-player tables and additional poker variants
- Push reminders
- Monetization gates around unvalidated curriculum content
- Solver-perfect push-fold or ICM claims

## Phase acceptance

- Each new concept connects teaching, randomized practice, review, progress,
  and the personal plan.
- Repeating a pack changes cards, positions, stacks, and action contexts—not
  only question order.
- Tournament advice always states the stack and table context that supports it.
- Opponent adjustments distinguish evidence from a small sample.
- New content remains readable on the smallest supported phone layouts.
- Localization completion and the full automated release gate pass.
