# RiverMind Spanish (Latin America, `es-419`) style guide and poker glossary

**Status:** Approved for Phase 19 first-draft translation. Native poker-language
review is **pending** and remains an owner gate; this guide is what that reviewer
reviews against. First-draft content must not ship past the language picker
until that review signs off.

**Audience:** Latin American Spanish speakers in Mexico, Colombia, Peru, Chile,
Argentina, Central America, and US Spanish speakers. One catalog serves all of
them; regional variants are deliberately avoided.

---

## 1. Voice and reading level

- Friendly, direct coach voice. RiverMind teaches; it never lectures and never
  mocks a wrong answer.
- Clear standard register, roughly a general-press reading level. Short sentences
  for instructions; one idea per sentence in lessons.
- Prefer concrete verbs over nominalizations: "decide", "compare", "pay" — not
  "realize the decision-making".
- No humor that depends on a single country's slang. Character titles (the
  multiway AI jokes) are the only place where the copy is deliberately playful,
  and they are rewritten per language rather than translated word for word.
- Product name is always "RiverMind". Never translate it.

## 2. Formality and second person

- Use **tú** consistently. Never usted (stiff for a learning game), never vos
  (regionally marked).
- Imperatives for actions: "Toca", "Elige", "Desliza". Avoid the formal
  subjunctive ("tenga a bien") and voseo forms ("elegí", "tocá").
- Possessives are natural: "tu mano", "tu rango". Do not over-repeat "tu" where
  Spanish drops it.

## 4. Retained English shorthand (do not translate)

`All-in`, `flop`, `turn`, `river`, `board` (in compact copy; "tablero" in prose),
`3-bet`, `4-bet`, `C-bet`, `SPR`, `EV`, `ICM`, `BB` (only in the seat glossary
where it names a seat, never as an amount), `Sit & Go`, `UTG`, `MP`, `CO`, `BTN`,
`SB`, `BB`, `K` (thousand abbreviation in `1.2K`), `RiverMind`.

Suit symbols are never translated: ♠ ♥ ♦ ♣ with ranks `A K Q J 10 9 8 7 6 5 4 3 2`.
Card strings keep English source order and spacing: "A♠ K♠", "Q♠ J♠ 10♠".

## 6. Grammar: counts, genders, articles

- Numbers up to and including twenty are written out in lesson prose
  ("dieciséis outs"); numbers in UI copy, amounts, and percentages stay numeric.
- Plural API: catalogs provide `one`/`other` forms for count-bearing keys
  ({{count}} big blind/big blinds → "ciega grande"/"ciegas grandes"). Never
  hardcode singular/plural in a screen.
- Watch noun gender: "el bote", "la ciega", "el color", "la escalera", "el par",
  "la mano", "el rango". Articles agree with the noun, not with "carta".
- "Jugador(es)", "decisión(es)", "mano(s)": use the plural API, never "(s)".
- Percentages: `40%` — no space, no "por ciento" in compact copy; "por ciento"
  allowed in lesson prose when the sentence reads better.
- Decimal amounts in big blinds keep the English source value ("2.5"): write
  "2.5" not "2,5" so interpolated poker facts stay identical to the source.

## 7. Numbers, punctuation, capitalization

- Thousands separator stays `,` (2,000 fichas) and decimal stays `.` for chips,
  big-blind amounts, and percentages: these are language-neutral poker notation
  (documented in the registry and inventory), not missing localization.
- Sentence case for buttons and titles: "Igualar y ver el flop". Only proper
  nouns and the first word capitalize.
- Questions open with ¿ and close with ?.
- Ellipsis: use the single character "…" not three periods.
- Quotation marks: «» never; use "" for quoted UI, "" for quoted speech.
- No terminal period on buttons, tabs, or single-line labels; full sentences in
  body copy take one.

## 8. Accessibility phrasing (TalkBack/VoiceOver)

- Labels are short noun phrases or clear verbs, read in one breath:
  "Igualar 20 fichas", not "Botón que sirve para igualar la cantidad de 20".
- State comes before action: "Plegado, Ruta de aprendizaje, encabezado".
- Percentages read naturally: "40 por ciento" is what the screen reader says,
  so write the label as "40% completo" — never "40 por ciento completo" in the
  string itself.
- Avoid symbol-only labels; name the suit when a card matters: "As de picas".
- Live announcements: "Mara sube a 40 fichas" — actor, action, amount, in that
  order, matching the table announcement order in English.
- No emoji inside accessibility labels.

## 9. Compact-control length guidance

Spanish runs 20–35% longer than English. Do not shorten blindly; compact labels
that exceed their slot are recorded for device review (the Phase 19 record lists
them).

| Surface | Target | Hard ceiling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Table action buttons | ≤ 10 chars | 14 |
| Seat plaques / status pills | ≤ 12 chars | 16 |
| Tab labels | ≤ 12 chars | 14 |
| Difficulty names | one word | 12 |
| Chip/bet amounts | numeric only | — |

Approved compact forms: "Igualar" (7), "Subir" (5), "Apostar" (7), "Pasar" (5),
"Retirarse" (9), "All-in" (6). If a control still overflows on device, file it
as a layout defect — do not re-translate to fit.

## 10. Banned literal translations and false friends

- "calle" for street — LatAm players know flop/turn/river; "calle" reads Spain.
- "asistir" for "attend a table" — false friend; use "unirse".
- "realizar" as filler for "do/make" — prefer concrete verbs.
- "pote" — that is Portuguese; Spanish is "bote".
- "draw" → "dibujar/arrastre" — nonsense; "proyecto".
- "out" → "afuera/fuera" — nonsense; "out/outs" retained.
- "actualmente" ≠ "actually" — means "currently".
- "apostar" vs "bet" in the sense of risk-taking prose: "arriesgar" when no bet
  is placed.
- "check" → "cheque" (bank check) — never.
- "educación" ≠ "education" in the polite-behavior sense.
- "éxito" means "success", not "exit" — exits are "salir".
- "sorteo" for tournament draw/seating — use "sorteo de asientos" only for
  seating randomization; never translate "draw" as "sorteo".
- Literal "ciegas grandes" → "grandes ciegas" (adjective order): wrong.
- Machine-converted Spain Spanish ("vosotros", " ordenador", "móvil"): banned.

## 11. Review contract

First drafts in this repository are machine-assisted and explicitly marked
`// DRAFT: awaiting qualified native es-419 poker-language review` in each
generated catalog header. Native review must cover, at minimum: the glossary
above, all lesson and scenario poker facts, consent and deletion copy, coach
output language instructions, and the compact-control label list. One qualified
reviewer may fill both the native-language and poker-knowledge roles per scope
§L4. Approval is recorded in `docs/PHASE_19_EXECUTION_RECORD.md` before the
language is shown past the language picker in a release build.
